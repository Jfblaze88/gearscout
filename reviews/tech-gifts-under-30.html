Eight tech gifts under $30 that don’t end up in a drawer

Cheap gadget gifts fail for one reason: they solve a problem the person doesn't have. Each of these is picked for someone specific — and every one is under $30.

Updated September 2026 · 8 picks · from $18.39

There is a version of the sub-$30 gadget gift that everyone has received at least once: a thing that seemed clever in the listing, got opened, got used twice, and now lives in a drawer with the charging cable still coiled in the box. It is not really a price problem. Plenty of genuinely good technology costs less than a takeaway for two.

It is a targeting problem. A cheap gadget works as a gift when it removes a small friction the person already complains about — cables everywhere, keys never findable, a laptop with two ports, a TV too old to stream. It fails when it is bought for the category rather than the person. So this list is organised by who each thing is for rather than by what it is.

What $30 does and doesn't buy: accessories are fully solved at this price — chargers, trackers, hubs, plugs and streaming sticks are simple devices and the cheap ones work. Anything with a screen, a motor, or a battery doing heavy lifting is where $30 starts buying something disappointing. Every pick below sits on the right side of that line.

Common questions

Can you get a decent tech gift for under $30?

Yes, but the category matters more than the budget. Accessories are fully solved at this price because they're simple devices. Anything with a screen, a motor, or a battery doing heavy work is where $30 buys something disappointing.

Why do cheap gadget gifts end up unused?

Usually because they solve a problem the recipient doesn't have. A gadget gift succeeds when it removes a small daily friction the person already complains about — which is why picking for the person beats picking by price.

Are noise cancelling headphones under $30 any good?

They'll flatten steady low-frequency noise — engine drone, office hum — which covers most commuting and study use. They won't match headphones costing several times more on sudden or high-frequency sound. Set expectations there and they're a strong gift.

Do Bluetooth tracker tags work with Android?

The MFi-certified tags here use Apple's Find My network, so they're for iPhone users. Check which phone the recipient carries first — it's the single most common mistake with tracker tags as a gift.
