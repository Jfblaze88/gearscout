6 camping gadgets under $60 that actually earn their space

Light, power, airflow, and bug control — the four things that decide whether a night in a tent is comfortable or just survivable. Every pick here stays under $60.

Updated September 2026 · 6 picks · Outdoor & Camping

Camping gear has a spending curve that stops being worth it much earlier than the marketing suggests. Past about $60 an item, most of what you are buying is weight savings — grams shaved off a lantern, a fan, a battery. That trade is real if you are carrying everything on your back for four days. It is close to meaningless if you are driving to the site and lifting the cooler out of the trunk.

So this list caps at $60 and focuses on the categories that change how the trip actually feels: how well you can see after dark, whether your phone survives to the second night, whether the tent is bearable at 2am in August, and how many mosquitoes are involved. Six picks, each one covering a distinct job. Prices are what the Amazon listing showed when the pick was added.

Common questions

How much should you spend on camping gadgets?

For car camping and weekend trips, the useful spend is concentrated under $60 an item. Light, power, and airflow are all solved at that price. Above it you are mostly buying weight savings — which matters a lot for backpacking and very little for anything you drive to.

Do you need a power bank if you already have a car charger?

A car charger only works while the engine is running or the battery has charge to spare. A power bank keeps phones, headlamps, and a rechargeable lantern topped up away from the vehicle — which is where most of a camping trip actually happens.

Are digital night vision goggles worth it for camping?

They are a want, not a need. A headlamp does more for less money. Night vision earns its place if you walk trails after dark, watch wildlife, or want to move around a site without a beam announcing you to everything nearby.

Does a bug zapper work at a campsite?

A plug-in zapper needs power, so it suits a campground with hookups, an RV, or a patio rather than a backcountry tent site. Where there is an outlet, it clears the area without spraying anything on your skin.
